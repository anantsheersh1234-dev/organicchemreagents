Identification Tests

Reagents used, positive observation, equation & drawn mechanism

Tollens' Test
Silver Mirror Test
Aldehyde Test
▾
Reagent
  • Ammoniacal AgNO₃ — i.e. [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ complex ("Tollens' reagent")
Observation
  • Aldehydes give a bright silver mirror on test-tube walls; ketones show no reaction
Drawn Mechanism — Redox
Aldehyde's carbonyl O attacks Ag⁺ while hydride-type electrons flow from C–H, oxidising –CHO to –COO⁻.
➜electron transfer
RCOO⁻ + Ag↓(mirror) + NH₃ + H₂O
Fehling's Test
Cu²⁺–Tartrate Complex
Aldehyde Test
▾
Reagent
  • Fehling A (CuSO₄ solution) + Fehling B (alkaline Na-K tartrate) mixed fresh
Observation
  • Aliphatic aldehydes → brick-red Cu₂O precipitate; aromatic aldehydes (e.g. benzaldehyde) give no reaction
Drawn Mechanism
Aldehyde is oxidised to carboxylate; Cu²⁺ is reduced to Cu⁺ (as Cu₂O).
➜
RCOO⁻ + Cu₂O↓ (brick-red)
2,4-DNP Test
Brady's Reagent
Carbonyl Test
▾
Reagent
  • 2,4-Dinitrophenylhydrazine in methanol/H₂SO₄
Observation
  • Any aldehyde or ketone gives a yellow/orange precipitate (hydrazone) — a general carbonyl test
Drawn Mechanism — Nucleophilic Addition–Elimination
Terminal NH₂ attacks carbonyl C → carbinolamine intermediate.
➜−H₂O
R₂C=N–NH–Ar (yellow/orange ppt)
Iodoform Test
I₂ / NaOH
Methyl Ketone Test
▾
Reagent
  • I₂ dissolved in NaOH (generates NaOI in situ)
Observation
  • Detects CH₃CO– group, or CH₃CH(OH)– (oxidised first) → yellow crystalline CHI₃ precipitate with characteristic odour
Drawn Mechanism — Haloform Reaction
OH⁻ removes an α-H; enolate attacks I₂ (×3) replacing all 3 H's on methyl carbon.
➜⁻OH attacks C=O

Lucas Test
conc. HCl + anhyd. ZnCl₂
Alcohol Test
▾
Reagent
  • ZnCl₂ (Lewis acid catalyst) dissolved in conc. HCl
